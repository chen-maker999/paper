\section{End-to-End Evaluation on ALFWorld}
\label{app:alfworld}
We play the 134 unseen-split games of ALFWorld (text version, TextWorld PDDL
files) with \texttt{qwen3.8-flash} (thinking disabled, temperature 0, at most 50
steps). At every step the history is compressed to a budget of 600 or 1200
tokens (task and history; the fixed system prompt is not charged), measured on
the rendered prompt. SKC uses a domain extractor that writes the latest
contents and open/closed state of each receptacle, the held object and
cleaned/heated/cooled objects to the ledger; it compacts only once the history
exceeds the budget and makes no LLM calls. The rolling summary folds the oldest
steps into a running summary written by the same model (one extra call each
time the budget overflows). Success is paired per game; p-values are exact
McNemar tests of SKC against each method.

We therefore make no end-to-end claim: on short-horizon household tasks the
state-keeping advantage measured by the probes does not translate into a
measurable success-rate gain.

\section{\textsc{StateTrack} Details}
\label{app:synthetic}

The generator produces agent-like trajectories with three key families:
\emph{pinned} constraints stated in the task and occasionally revised by the
user ($p=0.004$ per key and step), \emph{slow} facts rewritten rarely
($p=0.03$), and \emph{fast} status variables rewritten often ($p=0.25$). Writes
appear as \texttt{key = value} lines inside tool outputs (80\%) or agent notes
(20\%), among log-like distractor lines (lognormal, mean 25 lines per output).
Values are unique, so the ideal reader is exact. Unless stated otherwise we use
150 steps, 4 pinned, 12 slow and 6 fast keys, 30 seeds, and probe all keys at
three checkpoints. The ledger-based compressor here is SKC-basic (there is no
training corpus for a utility model); ``+BM25'' replaces its backward extension
by BM25 selection.
